The Exit Distribution of the Upper Half-Plane Is Cauchy

Run a two-dimensional Brownian motion from z₀ = x₀ + i y₀. Its vertical component is certain to hit 0, and the horizontal position at that moment is the exit position XT. Sample many times and watch the histogram settle onto the Cauchy density.

How to read it

  1. The upper half-plane has only the real axis as its boundary. The vertical component Y is a one-dimensional Brownian motion and almost surely hits 0, so the path must leave through the real axis and nowhere else.
  2. X and Y are independent, so T is determined by Y alone, and the position of X at time T is distributed as N(x₀, T). T itself is heavy-tailed (sometimes extremely long), so the variance is random, which produces exit points very far away.
  3. The histogram follows the solid line, with a much heavier tail than the dashed one. In the fraction readout under the histogram, the Cauchy theoretical value is 12.6%, while under a normal distribution it is almost 0.
  4. “Fast sample” does not simulate step by step; it uses the formula XT = x₀ + y₀·N′/N directly (N, N′ independent standard normals). It has the same distribution as the path simulation, and the exit-position statistics mix the two together.
