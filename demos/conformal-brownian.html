Conformal Invariance of Brownian Motion

Take the conformal map φ(z) = (z + a)/(1 + a z). It maps the unit disk bijectively onto itself but sends 0 to a. On the left is a Brownian path Bt in the disk; on the right is its image φ(Bt). Switch clocks and watch the motion on the right turn back into a uniform Brownian motion.

D: Brownian motion Bt in the disk

Background: warm where |φ′| is large, cool where |φ′| is small.

Original time t = 0.000Current |φ′(B)|² = 1.00

φ(D): image path φ(Bt)

The grid is the image of the left grid; lines still cross at right angles.

New time At = 0.000 

How the two clocks relate

Horizontal axis: original time t. Vertical axis: new time At. The slope of the curve is |φ′(Bt)|².

How to read it

  1. Each dot is a sample point on the path. Blue dots are in the left panel, pink dots in the right panel; denser dots mean slower motion.
  2. With the original clock, the dots on the left are evenly spaced. On the right the spacing varies, so φ(B) is not a uniform-speed Brownian motion.
  3. With the new clock, each step is given only δ/|φ′|² of time. The spacing on the right becomes uniform everywhere, while on the left it varies. This is the time change in the theorem.
  4. The distribution of exit points does not depend on the clock. The exit points on the right are exactly the images of those on the left; after a few hundred, the left ones look uniform while the right ones crowd toward the side where the start point a lies.
